Where every piper begins

First, no bagpipes. And that’s good news.

Every piper you have ever heard — including the ones playing in front of thousands — started the same way: on the practice chanter.

The chanter is a small, quiet, recorder-like instrument where all the real learning happens: fingering, grace notes, embellishments, and the tunes themselves. It is inexpensive, it will not annoy your neighbors, and it means you do not need to own — or be strong enough to blow — a full set of pipes on day one.

When your technique is ready, you will move to the full instrument with a teacher who has guided many students across exactly that bridge — from first scale to first parade.

The craft

What lessons cover.

Piping is equal parts technique, music, and instrument wrangling. Lessons build all three, at a pace set by you — privately or in a group, whichever suits you best.

Technique & embellishments

Clean fingering is everything on the pipes. Grace notes, doublings, birls, throws — taught the traditional way, correctly, from the start.

Repertoire & expression

Marches, strathspeys, reels, jigs, hornpipes, and slow airs — plus how to phrase them so a tune becomes music, not just notes.

Tone, tuning & care

Blowing steady, tuning drones, reeds, and maintenance — the craft of making the whole instrument sing, and keeping it that way.

For future pipers

Questions new students ask.

Do I need to own bagpipes to start lessons?

No — and you shouldn’t buy pipes yet. Every piper begins on the practice chanter, a small, quiet instrument where fingering and technique are learned before the full bagpipe ever enters the picture. Your teacher will advise on what to buy, and what to avoid, when the time comes.

How long does it take to learn the bagpipes?

Honest answer: it is a marathon, not a sprint. Most students spend several months on the practice chanter building clean fingering before moving to the full instrument, and the pipes reward steady, regular practice over years. A good teacher makes every stage musical — you’ll be playing real tunes sooner than you might think.

Am I too old (or too young) to start?

Adult beginners start the pipes all the time, and younger students can begin as soon as their hands can cover the chanter comfortably — your teacher can gauge that at a first meeting. Motivation matters far more than age.

Where do lessons take place, and how are they scheduled?

Lessons are arranged directly between you and your teacher — timing, frequency, and format that fit real life. Reach out through the inquiry form or by phone to talk through what would work for you in the Asheville area.
