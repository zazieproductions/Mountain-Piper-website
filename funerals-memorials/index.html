When words are not enough, the pipes speak.

Ways the pipes can serve

Honoring a life, in the way that fits your family.

Every family grieves differently, and every service should reflect the person it remembers. The pipes can play a leading role or a quiet one — at the church, the cemetery, or a gathering afterward.

At the funeral service

Slow airs as guests gather, a hymn within the service, or piping as the congregation departs — placed where your officiant and family feel it belongs.

At the graveside

The most time-honored role of all. A piper beginning from a distance as the family gathers, and a final air — often Amazing Grace — to close the committal.

Celebrations of life

For memorial gatherings with a different spirit — music that remembers a life fully lived, from laments to the tunes your loved one actually danced to.

Dress and volume can be toned down for quiet services — just ask.

Handled with care

A piper your family can lean on.

Planning a service happens quickly, often within days, and usually while grieving. The last thing a family needs is one more detail to manage. Mountain Piper keeps this side of things simple: one conversation, clear coordination with your funeral director, and a piper who arrives early, prepared, and calm.

Tunes are chosen with the same care. Amazing Grace is the most requested, and for good reason; slow airs such as Highland Cathedral and Going Home sit beautifully on the pipes as well. If there is a melody that mattered to your loved one, ask — if the instrument can carry it, it will be honored.

  • Direct coordination with your funeral home or officiant
  • Quiet arrival well before guests or the family procession
  • Guidance on tunes, including family requests where the pipes allow
  • Full Highland dress, or a simpler presentation for quiet services
  • Piping at the service, the graveside, or both

Practical matters

Questions families ask.

How quickly can a bagpiper be arranged for a funeral?

Funerals and memorials often come together within days, and Mountain Piper treats these inquiries with special urgency. Reach out as soon as you have a date, time, and place — by phone if the service is soon — and you’ll get a prompt, honest answer about whether your piper can be there.

Can the piper play Amazing Grace and other hymns?

Yes. Amazing Grace is the most requested tune at services, alongside slow airs and hymns that sit naturally on the pipes, such as Highland Cathedral and Going Home. If your family has a particular tune in mind, ask — if it suits the instrument, it can be honored.

Will the piper coordinate with the funeral home?

Yes. Mountain Piper will confirm timing and placement directly with your funeral director or officiant — when to begin, where to stand, and how the family wishes the music to enter and leave the service — so on the day there is nothing for you to manage.

Can the pipes play at the graveside only?

Absolutely. Many families ask for piping at the committal alone — often the piper begins from a distance as the family gathers, draws closer through the tune, and finishes with a final air as the service ends. Piping at both the funeral and the graveside can also be arranged.
